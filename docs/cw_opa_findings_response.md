Excellent work from CC. A few comments on the findings:

**The small-grid CW trick is clever.** Rather than a nanosecond flat-top pulse on a huge grid, using N=256 with a bandwidth spanning 775–1560 nm means each bin is a ~780 GHz CW tone, the time window is ~1.3 ps, and the solver is trivially fast. The "pulse" wraps around cyclically, so there are no edge effects — it's effectively a periodic steady-state solution. This is a much better approach than what I suggested in the brief. The 4× upsampling anti-aliasing requirement setting the floor at N=64 is a good catch.

**The poling period validation is reassuring.** SNOW's 4.4–5.0 µm landing within the literature range of 3.5–4.8 µm, with the systematic trend (thicker films → longer Λ) going in the right direction, is about as good as you can expect from an effective-index method without full 2D mode solving. The 700 nm film at 5.0 µm being on the high end is consistent with weaker confinement → dispersion closer to bulk → longer QPM period. Using it with the existing X0 calibration is the right call — internal consistency matters more than matching a specific paper's geometry.

**The literature gain numbers are the critical sanity check.** The Mishra result is the anchor: 23.5 dB phase-sensitive gain at 110 mW pump in 14 mm. That implies a phase-insensitive gain of roughly 11–12 dB (PS gain ≈ 2× PI gain in dB). For our simulation at similar pump power and length, we should land in that neighborhood. If we get 3 dB or 40 dB, X0 is wrong.

The Xin simulation at 71.5 dB is an outlier that illustrates the saturation issue we hit in the bulk notebook — at 0.5–3 W in 20 mm, you're deep into the regime where vacuum fluctuations self-seed to saturation. Their 0.3 dB/cm loss is what keeps it from going completely nonlinear. It's a useful upper-bound reference but not a calibration target.

**One physics point CC should be aware of for Cell 6 (phase-sensitive operation).** The phase that matters is θ = φ_pump − φ_signal − φ_idler, and this evolves along z due to any residual phase mismatch Δk. Even with QPM, the mismatch isn't exactly zero — it's zero for the designed wavelength pair at the designed temperature, but finite for any detuning. So the phase-sensitive gain will also depend on crystal length modulo the coherence length, not just on the input phase. For the perfectly phase-matched case, the input phase maps cleanly through the entire crystal and the Cell 6 plot will show the expected sinusoidal gain variation. But if CC tries it at a slightly detuned wavelength, the gain oscillates along z as well, which could wash out the phase sensitivity. Worth noting in the notebook — it's why real phase-sensitive OPAs require careful dispersion engineering.

**One concern not raised:** the 775 nm pump is above the two-photon absorption edge for LiNbO₃ (bandgap ~4 eV, so two-photon absorption kicks in below ~620 nm). So TPA shouldn't be a problem at 775 nm. But photo-refractive effects might be — at room temperature, 775 nm is short enough to excite carriers from defect states in congruent LN. In a real device, people either use MgO-doped LN (suppresses photorefractive damage) or operate at elevated temperature (>80°C). SNOW probably doesn't model photorefractive effects, so this is just a notebook annotation rather than a simulation parameter — but worth mentioning so readers know the simulation idealizes away a real experimental challenge.
