# Non-Degenerate OPA Tutorial — Physics Brief for CC

## Purpose

Build a new Jupyter notebook that simulates a **realistic, non-degenerate,
single-pass OPA in PPLN**, in contrast to the degenerate synch-pumped OPO of
Tutorial 7. The goal is to show what a practical parametric amplifier looks
like when it's doing something useful: generating tunable mid-IR light for
molecular spectroscopy.

The output should look *orderly* — clean amplified signal pulse, clean idler
pulse at a well-defined mid-IR wavelength, controlled spectral bandwidth. This
is the workhorse configuration, not the exotic broadband-degenerate regime.

---

## Use Case

**Mid-infrared generation for C–H stretch spectroscopy.**

Hydrocarbons have strong fundamental vibrational absorptions in the 3.2–3.5 µm
region. No convenient laser emits there directly. The standard approach: start
with a 1.064 µm Nd:YAG-derived pump (ubiquitous, cheap, reliable), seed with an
erbium-fiber source near 1.5 µm, and let energy conservation produce an idler in
the mid-IR.

---

## Wavelength Geometry

Energy conservation:  1/λ_p = 1/λ_s + 1/λ_i

| Wave   | Wavelength | Role                                   |
|--------|------------|----------------------------------------|
| Pump   | 1.064 µm  | From Nd:YAG (frequency-doubled source is also common, but let's use the fundamental) |
| Signal | 1.507 µm  | Seeded from erbium fiber oscillator    |
| Idler  | 3.617 µm  | Generated by the OPA — this is the useful output |

Check: 1/1.064 = 0.93985;  1/1.507 = 0.66357;  1/3.617 = 0.27647;
0.66357 + 0.27647 = 0.94004 ≈ 0.93985  ✓  (small rounding; pick exact values
that close perfectly, or let SNOW enforce energy conservation on the grid)

## PPLN Crystal Parameters

### Poling period

The QPM condition is:

    1/Λ = n_p/λ_p − n_s/λ_s − n_i/λ_i

For these wavelengths in congruent LiNbO₃ at ~150 °C, the poling period will be
approximately **29–31 µm**. (The exact value depends on which Sellmeier model
SNOW uses — let the code compute it from the model's own refractive indices.
Ideally, include a cell that calculates and prints the exact phase-matched Λ for
the target wavelengths at the chosen temperature.)

### Crystal length

**20 mm** — a standard commercially available PPLN length. Long enough for
substantial gain, short enough that group velocity mismatch doesn't completely
wreck the pulse.

### Temperature

**150 °C** as baseline — comfortably above photorefractive damage threshold
(which is a real operational concern in LiNbO₃ below ~100 °C at 1 µm
wavelengths) and within the range of standard PPLN ovens.

### Nonlinear coefficient

d₃₃ ≈ 27 pm/V for LiNbO₃. With first-order QPM, d_eff = 2·d₃₃/π ≈ 17.2 pm/V.
SNOW may handle this internally given the material model; verify it's using the
right value.

## Pulse Parameters

### Pump pulse

- **Wavelength:** 1.064 µm
- **Pulse duration:** 10 ps FWHM (Gaussian or sech² — Gaussian is fine)
- **Pulse energy:** Start with **1 µJ**, scan up to **10 µJ**
- **Beam size:** Not critical if SNOW is 1D (propagation axis only), but if
  needed, assume ~100 µm beam waist (1/e² radius), giving peak intensity
  ~0.3 GW/cm² at 1 µJ — well below PPLN damage threshold for ps pulses
- **Chirp:** Zero initially (transform-limited)

### Seed pulse

- **Wavelength:** 1.507 µm
- **Pulse duration:** 10 ps FWHM (matched to pump for good temporal overlap)
- **Pulse energy:** **1 nJ** — six orders of magnitude below pump (realistic
  for a fiber oscillator seed after filtering/attenuation)
- **Chirp:** Zero initially

### Idler

- Not seeded — it will be generated from vacuum by the parametric process
- Should appear at ~3.617 µm in the output

## Notebook Progression

Build up one effect at a time so each cell teaches something.

### Cell 1: Phase-Matching Landscape

Before running any propagation, compute and plot the QPM tuning curve:

- **Plot A:** For a fixed poling period (the one computed for our target
  wavelengths at 150 °C), plot signal and idler wavelength vs. crystal
  temperature. This shows the tuning range available by temperature alone.
  
- **Plot B:** For a fixed temperature (150 °C), plot signal and idler wavelength
  vs. poling period. This shows why people use multi-grating crystals.

**Expected behavior:** Two smooth curves (signal and idler) that move apart as
you go away from degeneracy, connected by energy conservation. At each
temperature, there's exactly one signal/idler pair that satisfies both energy
conservation and QPM simultaneously.

### Cell 2: Single-Pass OPA — Low Gain

Run the propagation with pump energy = 1 µJ.

**Plot:** Input and output spectra (pump, signal, idler all on one axis);
input and output temporal profiles.

**Expected behavior:**
- Signal amplified modestly (maybe 10–20 dB gain)
- Idler appears as a new spectral peak at 3.617 µm
- Pump shows slight depletion at center of pulse (where it overlaps the seed)
- Temporal profiles remain clean — signal is a slightly amplified copy of the
  seed, idler has similar duration
- Spectral bandwidths are narrow and well-defined

This should look *qualitatively different* from the degenerate OPO output — no
octave-spanning spectrum, no temporal modulation mess. Comment on this contrast
in the notebook.

### Cell 3: Single-Pass OPA — High Gain / Saturation

Increase pump energy to 5–10 µJ (or whatever drives the system into saturation).

**Expected behavior:**
- Signal gain saturates — back-conversion begins (energy flows back from
  signal+idler into pump)
- Signal pulse may develop a temporal dip at its peak (the center converts
  first, then back-converts while the wings are still amplifying)
- Idler shows corresponding structure
- Pump develops a deep depletion hole
- Spectra may broaden due to self-phase modulation and gain-driven spectral
  reshaping

**Plot:** Same format as Cell 2 but overlay low-gain and high-gain results to
show the transition. A plot of signal energy vs. pump energy (a gain curve)
would be very instructive — it should show exponential growth at low pump,
rolling over to saturation, then oscillating if you push hard enough
(back-conversion cycles).

### Cell 4: Group Velocity Mismatch Effects

This is the key physics unique to ultrafast OPA.

The three waves travel at different group velocities in PPLN. For our
wavelengths, the ordering is approximately:

    v_g(pump, 1.064 µm) < v_g(signal, 1.5 µm) < v_g(idler, 3.6 µm)

(Verify with SNOW's dispersion model — the exact ordering depends on the
Sellmeier coefficients.)

The GVM between pump and signal determines how quickly they walk off each other
temporally. For a 20 mm crystal, the walk-off may be comparable to or larger
than the 10 ps pulse duration — which means the signal "slides through" the
pump, sampling different pump intensity slices. This:

- Limits the effective interaction length
- Shapes the output pulse duration (can be shorter or longer than the input
  depending on geometry)
- Determines the gain bandwidth (shorter effective interaction = broader gain BW)

**Demonstrate by comparing:**
- 10 ps pulses in 20 mm crystal (GVM significant)
- 10 ps pulses in 5 mm crystal (GVM less significant)
- 1 ps pulses in 20 mm crystal (GVM dominant — the pulses walk through each
  other completely)

**Plot:** Temporal snapshots of all three pulses at several points along the
crystal (if SNOW supports intermediate outputs). This is the most physically
instructive visualization in the whole notebook — seeing the signal slide past
the pump makes the GVM concept visceral.

### Cell 5: Temperature Tuning

Using the baseline pump energy (Cell 2 regime, low-to-moderate gain), sweep the
crystal temperature across a range (say 100–200 °C) and plot the output idler
center wavelength vs. temperature.

**Expected behavior:** The idler tunes smoothly across a range in the mid-IR.
At each temperature, the gain bandwidth defines how spectrally narrow the output
is. Away from the designed phase-matching temperature, the gain drops (the QPM
condition is progressively less satisfied), so there's a usable tuning range
beyond which the device stops working.

**Plot A:** Idler spectrum at several temperatures overlaid (a waterfall or
stacked plot).

**Plot B:** Idler center wavelength and signal energy vs. temperature — shows
the tuning curve and the gain falloff.

### Cell 6: Seed Wavelength Detuning

Keep temperature fixed at 150 °C, but shift the seed wavelength away from the
perfectly phase-matched value (e.g., 1.48, 1.50, 1.507, 1.52, 1.55 µm).

**Expected behavior:** The gain drops as the seed moves away from the
phase-matched signal wavelength. The acceptance bandwidth of the crystal
(determined by crystal length and GVM) defines how far you can detune before
gain drops significantly. The idler wavelength shifts correspondingly (energy
conservation).

**Plot:** Gain (or output signal energy) vs. seed wavelength — this maps out
the parametric gain bandwidth of the device.

### Cell 7 (Optional): Two-Stage OPA

If the infrastructure supports it easily, chain two crystals: first stage with
low pump energy for clean pre-amplification, second stage with high pump energy
for power amplification. This is how real OPA systems work (TOPAS-like
architecture).

**Expected behavior:** Much higher total gain with less back-conversion than a
single high-gain stage, cleaner spectral and temporal profiles at high output
energy.

## What "Good" Output Looks Like

For reference, so CC knows when the simulation is behaving correctly:

| Parameter               | Low gain (1 µJ pump) | High gain (10 µJ pump) |
|-------------------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Signal gain             | 10–30 dB             | 40–60 dB              |
| Signal pulse duration   | ~8–12 ps             | ~5–10 ps (compressed by saturation) |
| Signal spectral BW      | ~1–5 nm              | ~5–20 nm (broadened)   |
| Idler center wavelength | ~3.62 µm             | ~3.62 µm              |
| Pump depletion          | < 10%                | 30–70%                |

These are rough targets — the exact numbers depend on SNOW's Sellmeier model and
propagation scheme. The qualitative behavior (clean pulses, defined spectral
peaks, idler appearing at the right wavelength) is more important than hitting
exact numbers.

## Notes for CC

1. **Sellmeier model:** Make sure SNOW is using a Sellmeier model valid for
   congruent LiNbO₃ in the 1–4 µm range. The standard references are
   Jundt (1997) for the extraordinary index, which covers this range well.
   If SNOW has multiple material models, pick the one that includes temperature
   dependence and is valid out to ~4 µm.

2. **Spectral window:** The simulation grid needs to cover at least 0.9–4.2 µm
   to capture pump, signal, and idler with room for spectral broadening. Check
   that the grid resolution is sufficient to resolve the narrowest spectral
   features.

3. **Temporal window:** Needs to be wide enough to accommodate GVM walk-off.
   For 20 mm of crystal, the pump-idler GVM could accumulate 10+ ps of delay.
   A window of ~50–100 ps should be safe.

4. **Absorption:** LiNbO₃ has non-trivial absorption beyond ~4 µm. If SNOW
   includes material absorption, you may see the idler losing energy. This is
   physically real and worth noting — it's one reason PPLN OPAs for wavelengths
   beyond ~4 µm often switch to other materials (PPKTP, orientation-patterned
   GaAs, etc.).

5. **Comparison to Tutorial 7:** Include a summary cell at the end that
   explicitly contrasts the non-degenerate OPA output (this notebook) with the
   degenerate OPO output (Tutorial 7). The visual difference is the lesson:
   same underlying physics (χ² parametric interaction in PPLN), radically
   different output character depending on configuration.

6. **Units and conventions:** Plot spectra in physically intuitive units — nm
   or µm on wavelength axis, linear or dB scale on power axis. Time domain in
   ps. Label everything. The Tutorial 7 plots used dBm/Hz, which is fine for
   spectral density but make sure the y-axis makes sense for the signal levels
   involved.
